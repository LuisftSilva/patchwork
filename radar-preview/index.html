03 · Regulation & Policy

Translate regulatory change into business impact.

A legal change matters because it can open a market, close a route, alter prescription behaviour, change import requirements or reshape who can manufacture and distribute.

Does this change the addressable market?
Does it change who may prescribe, import, manufacture or dispense?
Does it affect the product form that can legally reach patients?
Does it create a new B2B route even where retail flower is limited?
Spain · 2025→2026

Medical cannabis access does not equal a German-style flower market.

Spain created a regulated pathway around standardised cannabis preparations and hospital magistral formulations. That distinction matters when assessing export demand from Portugal.

Portugal ↔ Spain

Trade flow can be industrial even when patient access is narrow.

Material can move through authorised medical/scientific manufacturing, processing or re-export channels. A destination country is therefore not automatically the final patient market.

Germany · monitored

Prescription and distribution rules can change the commercial value of demand.

The Radar treats access-rule changes as market events because they can affect prescribing channels, distribution models and the sustainability of current growth.

Europe · continuous

Regulation is connected to market metrics.

The objective is not a legal-news archive: the objective is to observe whether regulatory changes are followed by measurable changes in patients, prescriptions, imports, prices, capacity or competition.
